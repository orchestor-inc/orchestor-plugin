# Content improvement cycle

1. Identify the exact page, user question, and observed citation/answer gap.
2. Read the current page and relevant cited evidence. Do not infer its contents
   from a title or search snippet.
3. Propose a specific change: which claim, section, entity explanation, or evidence
   should change, and why it addresses the observed need.
4. Preserve factual accuracy and the owner's voice. Do not invent testimonials,
   customers, statistics, product capabilities, or independent endorsements.
5. If editing is requested, prepare a reviewable draft through the user's available
   content tool. Orchestor analysis alone is not a publishing permission.
6. Publish only when the destination and content are authorized. Record what changed.
7. When follow-up measurement is requested, compare the same cohort and disclose
   confounders. A before/after difference alone is not causal proof.

**Incorrect:** promise a universal content formula or add unsupported facts to
make a page more likely to be cited.

**Correct:** make an evidence-backed change and define what observation would
support, contradict, or leave the hypothesis unresolved.
