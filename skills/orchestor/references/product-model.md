# Product relationships

A workspace is an authorization and data boundary. Projects organize work inside
it; they are not a replacement for workspace isolation between customers.

A brand identifies an organization and its comparison set. Topics and tags
organize prompts. A run requests an observation of a saved prompt on an eligible
consumer channel. Answers retain the resulting evidence. Reports aggregate that
evidence under a defined scope, date range, and set of filters.

Sources describe cited domains and pages. A citation is different from a brand
mention, and a search result is different from an adopted citation. Perception
records attributed brand characteristics; an attributed characteristic is not
necessarily positive sentiment. Shopping reports concern product appearances
and merchants, not proof of sales.

Issues, projects, milestones, initiatives, files, and views organize follow-up
work. External provider connections and MCP/CLI authentication are distinct from
the evidence itself. A connected provider does not prove that data has arrived.

Internal deployment, database maintenance, operator browser control, and company
credentials are outside these public product skills. Do not search for private
operator tools when a public command is unavailable. Report the actual capability
or permission gap and use the product documentation.
